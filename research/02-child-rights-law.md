## Key provisions to memorise

### Constitution
- **Art 21A** (86th Amendment 2002, in force 1.4.2010): free and compulsory education for **6–14** years, "in such manner as the State may by law determine". RTE Act is that law.
- **Art 45** (substituted by the 86th Amendment): the State shall endeavour to provide **ECCE** for all children **until they complete 6 years**.
- **Art 51A(k)** (Fundamental Duty, added by the 86th Amendment): the parent or guardian must provide opportunities for education to a child or ward aged **6–14**.
- Also: Art 15(3) (special provisions for children), **Art 24** (no child below 14 in factories, mines or hazardous work), Art 39(e)(f) (protecting childhood), Art 46 (education of weaker sections), Art 350A (primary education in the mother tongue).

### RTE Act 2009 (Act 35 of 2009; in force 1 April 2010)
- **s.2(c)** child = 6–14 years. **s.2(d)** child belonging to a disadvantaged group. **s.2(e)** weaker section. **s.2(ee)** child with disability (added 2012). **s.2(n)** school, of four kinds: govt, aided, specified category (KV/JNV/Sainik), and unaided.
- **s.3**: right to free and compulsory education in a neighbourhood school until elementary education is complete (Classes I–VIII). The 2012 proviso covers CWSN, including **home-based education** for severe or multiple disabilities.
- **s.4**: age-appropriate admission and **special training** for out-of-school children. **s.5**: right to transfer; the TC cannot be delayed.
- **s.6**: government must set up neighbourhood schools within 3 years. **s.8 / s.9**: duties of the appropriate government and the local authority. **s.10**: duty of parents. **s.11**: pre-school education (3–6 years).
- **s.12(1)(c)**: **25%** of seats in Class I (or pre-school) in unaided and specified-category schools go to weaker section and disadvantaged-group children from the neighbourhood. **s.12(2)**: the State reimburses these schools at its per-child cost.
- **s.13**: no **capitation fee** and no **screening procedure**. Penalty for a capitation fee: up to 10 times the fee. Penalty for screening: ₹25,000 for the first offence and ₹50,000 for each later one.
- **s.14**: admission cannot be denied for lack of age proof. **s.15**: admission cannot be denied (the extended admission period).
- **s.16** (substituted by the 2019 Amendment): regular exam in **Class 5 and Class 8** at the end of each year. A child who fails gets **additional instruction and a re-exam within 2 months**. If the child fails again, the appropriate government *may* allow the school to hold the child back. **No child may be expelled** until elementary education is complete. The original s.16 barred any holding back or expulsion up to Class VIII. The 2024 central rules apply this to Centre- and UT-run schools.
- **s.17**: **no physical punishment or mental harassment**. Breach leads to disciplinary action under the service rules.
- **s.18**: no school may run without **recognition**. Penalty up to ₹1 lakh, plus ₹10,000 per day while the breach continues. **s.19**: norms in the Schedule must be met within 3 years.
- **s.21**: **School Management Committee**. At least **3/4** of members are parents or guardians, with proportionate representation of disadvantaged and weaker-section parents, and **50% women**. **s.22**: School Development Plan.
- **s.23**: teacher qualifications set by the academic authority (**NCTE**). The 2017 Amendment let untrained in-service teachers qualify by 31.03.2019. **s.24**: duties of teachers (punctuality, completing the curriculum, assessing learning, parent meetings). **s.25**: PTR to be maintained.
- **s.27**: teachers may not be deployed for non-educational work except the **decennial census, disaster relief and elections** (local body, State legislature, Parliament). **s.28**: teachers barred from **private tuition**.
- **s.29**: curriculum and evaluation set by the academic authority (NCERT centrally, SCERT in States). It must follow constitutional values and aim at all-round development, activity-based learning and **mother tongue as the medium as far as practicable**. It must keep children free from fear, trauma and anxiety and use **comprehensive and continuous evaluation (CCE)**. Learning outcomes were added to the rules in 2017.
- **s.30**: no Board exam until elementary education is complete (s.30(1)), and a completion certificate (s.30(2)). Recheck s.30(1) in the consolidated text against the 2019 Amendment.
- **s.31**: **NCPCR/SCPCR** monitor RTE rights (under the CPCR Act 2005). **s.32**: grievance redressal by the local authority. **s.33 / s.34**: National and State Advisory Councils. **s.35**: power to issue guidelines and directions. **s.38**: rule-making power (Central Rules 2010, Delhi Rules 2011).
- **Schedule norms**: PTR **30:1** at primary and **35:1** at upper primary. **200 / 220** working days and **800 / 1000** instructional hours per year (primary / upper primary). Teachers work **45 hours a week** including preparation. A head teacher is required where enrolment is above 150 (primary) or 100 (upper primary).

### POCSO Act 2012 (as amended 2019) and POCSO Rules 2020
- **s.2(1)(d)**: child = under **18**. The Act is gender-neutral.
- **s.3/4** penetrative sexual assault: **minimum 10 years**, or a **minimum of 20 years** if the child is under 16 (2019). **s.5/6** aggravated penetrative sexual assault: **20 years to life, or death** (2019). **s.7/8** sexual assault: 3–5 years. **s.9/10** aggravated sexual assault: 5–7 years. **s.11/12** sexual harassment: up to 3 years. **s.13–15** child pornography (s.15, storage, was strengthened in 2019).
- s.9/s.5 list an offence by staff of an **educational institution** as *aggravated*.
- **s.19**: **mandatory reporting** to the SJPU or local police. **s.21**: failing to report is punishable with up to 6 months. If the **head of an institution** fails to report an offence by a subordinate, the penalty is up to **1 year**. **s.22**: false complaint (a child is not punished).
- **s.23**: media must not disclose the child's identity. **s.24**: statement recorded at the child's place, by a woman officer where possible, not in uniform. **s.28**: Special Courts. **s.29**: presumption of guilt. **s.35**: evidence recorded within **30 days** and trial completed within **1 year**. **s.39**: support persons. **s.44**: monitoring by NCPCR/SCPCR.
- **POCSO Rules 2020**: institutions in regular contact with children, including schools, must carry out **periodic police verification and background checks** of staff and give child-protection training (Rule 3). Rule 9 covers **interim compensation**. Rule 11 covers reporting pornographic material. Rule 12 covers monitoring. Look up the exact sub-rule numbers in the PDF.

### RPwD Act 2016 (CWSN)
- **21 specified disabilities**. **Benchmark disability** means at least 40%. **s.2(m)** defines **inclusive education**.
- **s.16**: duties of government-funded and recognised educational institutions. They must admit without discrimination, make buildings accessible and provide reasonable accommodation, suitable communication modes (Braille, sign language) and individual support, and detect specific learning disabilities early. **s.17**: specific measures, including a school-age survey **every 5 years**, teacher training, and free books and aids.
- **s.31**: free education for children with benchmark disabilities aged **6–18 years**. **s.32**: at least **5%** reservation in higher education. **s.34**: **4%** reservation in government posts.
- **Samagra Shiksha IE component**: covers CWSN from pre-primary to Class XII in government, aided and local-body schools. It funds identification camps, aids and appliances, Braille and large-print books, escort and transport allowances, a stipend for girls with disabilities, home-based education, and resource rooms. Take per-child amounts from the Framework PDF.

### JJ Act 2015, PCMA 2006, Child Labour Act
- **JJ Act**: s.2(12) child = under 18. s.2(13) child in conflict with law. s.2(14) child in need of care and protection. **s.15**: preliminary assessment of a 16–18-year-old accused of a heinous offence. s.27 Child Welfare Committee. s.4 JJ Board. **s.74**: child's identity must not be disclosed. **s.75**: cruelty to a child (higher penalty if committed by someone in charge of an organisation). **s.82**: corporal punishment in child care institutions. The 2021 amendment gave District Magistrates the power to issue adoption orders and more oversight.
- **PCMA 2006**: child = male under **21**, female under **18**. **s.3**: a child marriage is **voidable** at the option of the party who was a child, by petition within **2 years of reaching majority**. s.9: an adult male who marries a child gets up to **2 years RI and/or ₹1 lakh fine**. s.10 / s.11: penalties for solemnising and for promoting the marriage. s.12: void in certain cases (trafficking, force). s.13: injunctions. **s.16**: Child Marriage Prohibition Officers. The 2021 Bill to raise the minimum age for women to 21 lapsed with the 17th Lok Sabha.
- **Child and Adolescent Labour Act** (amended 2016): **no child under 14 in any occupation**, except helping a family enterprise after school hours or audio-visual entertainment (not circus). **Adolescents aged 14–18** are barred from hazardous occupations. Penalty: **6 months–2 years and/or ₹20,000–50,000**. Rehabilitation Fund. PENCIL portal.
